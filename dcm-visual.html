MeshDCM — concept visualisation

A triangulated compliance layer sitting inside the body structure: many small rigid cells, each filled with shear-thickening fluid and compressible gas pockets, sharing one impact between hundreds of load paths.

Zero-mobility cells

Grübler on a triangle gives M = 3(3−1) − 2(3) = 0. The cell cannot change shape without straining its members, so load leaves through all three nodes.

Centroidal sharing

A force through the centroid of an equilateral cell splits into three equal reactions of F/3 by symmetry.

Small cells, better odds

Effectiveness falls with the offset L from the centroid, and L scales with cell size — so the mesh is refined rather than enlarged.

Rate-dependent fill

STF flows at low shear and stiffens on impact; trapped gas pockets give the cell somewhere to go while pressure climbs.

Same structure, four different cars

Because the mesh lives in the body structure, the outer surface is free. Cell density, node spacing and panel material change with the job; the silhouette changes with taste.

Inside one cell

The design notes place the gas as pockets dispersed through the fluid, not a separate layer stacked under it — so that's how it's drawn here.

Design decisions vs. the earlier reference renders

The mesh sits inside the car. Earlier reference renders laid a black net over the paint, which reads as a cage bolted onto a finished car and does no visible work. Here the cells are read through the panels as facet lines, and shown in full only where the body is cut away. Structure visible at a seam reads as deliberate; structure draped over a door does not.

The name. Dynamic Compliance Mesh throughout — "compliance" is the derived behaviour, and the mesh is exploited at the centroids rather than being a centroidal mesh itself.

Gas as pockets, not a layer. The source derivation places small SF6 pockets dispersed between the STF. A stacked liquid-over-gas cross-section is a different, weaker device.

A flag on the chemistry. SF6 offers density, stability and compressibility, but it is the most potent greenhouse gas in wide use and is being phased out of switchgear, so a sealed automotive cell full of it is unlikely to clear regulation. The source notes already list the gas as a candidate — CO2, argon and fluoroketones are worth benchmarking against the same compressibility and shock-attenuation targets before the choice hardens.
